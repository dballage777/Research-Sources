# NOTES: A Christian (non-denominational) perspective on stress and anxiety self-help, and Bible quotation permissions

## Scope (from the user)
- Bring a respectful, non-denominational Christian framing to evidence-based stress and anxiety self-help (a follow-up to research/stress-anxiety-self-help-ai-chatbot-safety).
- Show how to pair Scripture with proven coping methods.
- Identify which Bible translations can be quoted freely (public domain) and which have quotation limits.
- Preferred sources: Christian counseling organizations, faith-and-mental-health research, and Bible publishers' permission pages.
- Defaults: all audiences, the most current resources, English, any region. Date: 2026-11-12.
- Not legal advice: for publications, confirm terms on the publisher's page at the time of use.

## Faith-and-mental-health findings
- Respectful framing and referral: the APA Foundation's "Mental Health: A Guide for Faith Leaders" (2nd ed., 2025 PDF) uses a Notice → Talk → Act model. It covers when to refer to professionals and crisis lines (988), and describes faith communities as partners in care, not replacements for it.
- Pairing Scripture with proven methods: Duke's Religiously-Integrated CBT (RCBT) has free Christian-version therapist and participant manuals. These add Scripture memorization, prayer and contemplation to standard CBT for depression in people with chronic illness. Meta-analyses (Anderson et al. 2015; Worthington et al. 2011; summarized via search) find faith-adapted CBT at least as effective as standard CBT, with somewhat larger spiritual-outcome gains, but the trials are small with allegiance-bias risk.
- Religion and mental health: the Koenig 2012 review (PMC) summarizes associations with lower depression and suicide and better well-being. Harvard Human Flourishing Program cohort studies link weekly service attendance to lower "deaths of despair". These studies are observational, so they do not show that attendance causes the benefit.
- Christian organizations: Mental Health Grace Alliance runs evidence-informed recovery groups. CAPS is a professional association integrating faith with psychological science. Hope for Mental Health (Saddleback) runs church support groups. NAMI FaithNet connects faith communities with NAMI resources. CCEF represents the biblical counseling tradition, which differs from the integration approach; it was a near-miss.
- Church context: Lifeway Research (2014; 2021 survey covered by Christianity Today) found that many pastors rarely address mental illness and many churches lack referral lists. A frequently quoted claim that "half of evangelicals believe prayer and Bible study alone can cure mental illness" traces to Lifeway's 2014 work; it was not re-verified on the original page.
- Respectful practice: the Society for the Advancement of Psychotherapy article on ethics says to respect clients' beliefs, assess religious and spiritual factors, and avoid imposing values. The guide also warns against implying that anxiety reflects weak faith; AACC material (blocked) notes guilt and judgment are common among anxious Christians.

## Disagreements and cross-checks
- ESV limit: secondary blogs claim 1,000 verses; Crossway's own page says 500 consecutive verses. The primary source was used.
- NKJV limit: some blogs say 250 verses; HarperCollins Christian's own page says 500 verses (≤25% of the work). The primary source was used.
- NLT: blogs say 500 verses (<25%). Hendrickson's NLT fair-use text agrees (print and eBook). Tyndale's own page was blocked.
- Integration vs biblical counseling: CAPS, Grace Alliance and the APA Foundation support combining faith with licensed, evidence-based care. CCEF-style biblical counseling emphasizes Scripture-centered counseling. Both are described respectfully; the list leans toward integration because of the user's request for "proven coping methods".

## Community findings (ANECDOTAL)
Reddit was blocked. Search snippets from pastors and authors (Steve Laube; MinistryWatch) describe confusion over Bible copyright and frustration with permission processes. The common practical advice is to use WEB, BSB or KJV for unrestricted quoting, or to stay under the publisher's limits with the correct notice.
