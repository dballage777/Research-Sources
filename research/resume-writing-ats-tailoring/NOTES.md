## Community findings (ANECDOTAL)
- Recruiters quoted in the Enhancv 25-recruiter study (vendor-run, small sample): 92% say their ATS does not auto-reject on formatting. The real gates are knockout questions (work authorization, licenses), recruiter keyword searches, and sheer volume. The "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS" claim has no traceable source.
- Career counselors (NACE "Ghostwritten Candidate", June 2026) report near-identical AI-generated resumes from different students, which is an immediate red flag to advisors and recruiters.
- Reddit and HN were blocked or thin, so no forum consensus was used.

## Disagreements and cross-checks
- File format: most 2025–2026 career centers (Utah, UVU, SIU) recommend a text-based PDF. Bellevue (2024, adapted from UVA) says .doc/.docx is safest. Santa Clara says to follow each application's instructions. Workday's own admin guide says parsing results vary by format and word order and recommends resumes without images. Consensus: use a simple, single-column, text-based file; follow posting instructions; check the parsed fields after upload.
- Does the ATS "rank" or "reject"? UCI's and UVU's guides describe ATS as scanning, scoring and ranking. Enhancv's recruiter survey and Huntr say most systems store, search and sort, and humans decide, with the exception of knockout questions. Workday's documentation confirms parsing only pre-fills fields for recruiter review. Both views appear in the list; the primary ATS-maker evidence supports "parse and search, rarely auto-reject".
- Scale of AI use and fraud: Greenhouse (Nov 2025, about 4,100 respondents) reports 41% of U.S. job seekers admit using prompt injection and 91% of recruiters have spotted deception. Robert Half (Nov 2025) reports 67% of HR leaders say AI-generated applications slow hiring. GCheck (2026, vendor) reports 93% of job seekers admit some embellishment. All are surveys from parties with commercial interests, so treat them as directional.
- AI screeners and AI-written resumes: arXiv 2509.00462 finds LLM screeners prefer resumes written by the same LLM. This is experimental evidence of a risk, not proof of how deployed ATS behave.
- Honesty: every career center reviewed (CMU, Georgia Tech, Fresno State, NACE) says to use AI only to edit your own true content, to verify every claim, and never to add skills or experience you don't have. SHRM and NACE sources note that background checks and interviews expose fabrication.
